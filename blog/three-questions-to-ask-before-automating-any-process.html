Three Questions to Ask Before Automating Any Process

July 19, 2026

Automation is appealing because it promises to save time. But automating the wrong process can create more problems than it solves. Before committing to any automation project, run it through these three questions first.

Question one: Is this process stable? If the way you handle this task changes frequently — different steps depending on the client, evolving requirements, or judgment calls that vary each time — automation will be brittle. You'll spend more time maintaining the automation than you save by running it. The best candidates for automation are tasks that follow roughly the same pattern every time, even if the specific inputs change.

Question two: What happens when it breaks? Every automated system will eventually produce an incorrect output. The question is whether that error is easy to catch and easy to fix, or whether it causes a chain reaction before anyone notices. Automating email drafts is low-risk because a person reviews before sending. Automating financial calculations with no review step is high-risk. Understand the blast radius before you build.

Question three: Will this free up time for higher-value work? Automation for its own sake doesn't help your business. The goal is to shift your team's time from repetitive tasks to work that requires judgment, creativity, or relationship-building. If the time saved just gets absorbed by other busywork, the automation didn't accomplish much. Have a plan for what your team will do with the hours they get back.

These questions aren't meant to discourage automation. They're meant to focus it. The businesses that get the most from AI are the ones that automate deliberately, starting with processes where the answers to all three questions point in the right direction.
