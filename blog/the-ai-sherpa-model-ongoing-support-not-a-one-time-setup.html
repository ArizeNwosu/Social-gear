The AI Sherpa Model: Ongoing Support, Not a One-Time Setup

September 12, 2026

A lot of AI consulting follows the same pattern: assess, recommend, deliver a report, and move on. The problem is that the report sits in a drawer. The tools get set up but not maintained. The team tries the new workflow for two weeks and then drifts back to old habits. Implementation without ongoing support is a recipe for expensive shelf-ware.

We use the term "AI Sherpa" deliberately. A sherpa doesn't just draw you a map of the mountain and wish you luck. They walk with you, adjust the pace, help you navigate obstacles, and make sure you actually reach the destination. That's the model we follow. Strategy is the starting point, but the real value is in the months that come after.

Ongoing support means different things at different stages. In the first few weeks, it means being available when the new workflow breaks down, when the AI produces an unexpected output, or when a team member has a question they're embarrassed to ask. In the months that follow, it means reviewing performance data, suggesting refinements, and identifying the next workflow to automate based on what you've learned.

It also means staying current with the tools. AI technology changes quickly. The tool that was the best choice six months ago might have a better alternative today. A sherpa relationship means you have someone who's tracking those changes and telling you when it matters — and when it doesn't. Most of the time, stability is more valuable than chasing the latest release.

The businesses that get lasting value from AI are the ones that treat it as an ongoing practice, not a one-time project. Having a guide who knows your business, understands your workflows, and is there when questions come up makes that practice sustainable.
