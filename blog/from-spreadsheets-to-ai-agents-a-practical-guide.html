From Spreadsheets to AI Agents: A Practical Guide

October 12, 2026

If your business runs on spreadsheets, you're closer to AI than you think. Every spreadsheet that tracks leads, calculates pricing, logs client information, or generates reports contains a set of rules — even if those rules only exist in the head of the person who built it. Those rules are the starting point for your first AI workflow.

The transition begins with documenting what the spreadsheet does. Not the formulas themselves, but the logic behind them. When a new row gets added, what happens? Who looks at it, and what do they do with the information? If a value crosses a threshold, what action follows? These are the decision points that an AI agent can eventually handle — but first you need to see them clearly.

The next step is identifying which parts of the spreadsheet workflow are manual. Are people copying data from emails into cells? Are they looking up information in one sheet and entering it in another? Are they sending a message or creating a document based on what the spreadsheet tells them? Each of those manual steps is a candidate for automation.

An AI agent built from spreadsheet logic doesn't look like a spreadsheet anymore. It might look like a form that automatically populates a database, a system that reads incoming emails and updates records, or a tool that generates a draft proposal based on the data you've already collected. The logic is the same; the execution is faster and less prone to human error.

The important thing is not to abandon the spreadsheet prematurely. Keep it running in parallel while the new system proves itself. Compare the outputs. Let your team build confidence that the agent handles the routine cases correctly. Then, gradually, let the spreadsheet become the backup rather than the primary system. The transition should feel natural, not forced.
