Why We Start Every Engagement with a Time Map

September 1, 2026

When a new client asks us where they should start with AI, our answer is always the same: let's look at how your time is being spent first. Not because we're stalling, but because the time map is the single most useful diagnostic we've found for identifying where AI will make the biggest difference.

A time map is a one-week snapshot of how your team spends their working hours. Each person logs their tasks, the approximate time each one takes, and the type of work involved — administrative, creative, communication, or operational. The exercise isn't scientific. It doesn't need to be precise down to the minute. It just needs to be honest.

What the map reveals, almost every time, is a gap between what people think they spend time on and what they actually spend time on. Business owners consistently underestimate the hours going to email, data entry, and scheduling. They overestimate the time going to strategic work, client relationship building, and creative problem-solving. The map closes that gap.

Once we have the map, the conversation changes. Instead of debating which AI tool is best in the abstract, we're looking at specific tasks that consume real hours. We can point to the rows that show ten hours a week on email and ask: what if a good AI draft cut that to four? We can identify the three hours a week spent copying data between systems and propose a workflow that eliminates it.

The time map also protects against the temptation to automate everything at once. By showing exactly where the biggest returns are, it gives you and your team a clear, defensible starting point. You build one workflow, see the results, and decide what comes next. It's a small investment of time that shapes everything that follows.

We'll guide your team through the time map process and help you interpret the results. It's a straightforward way to start.
