Building Trust with AI: Start with What People Already Do

November 3, 2026

Trust in AI isn't built by explaining how the technology works. It's built by showing people that the technology can do something they already know how to evaluate. When your team can look at an AI output and say "that's right" or "that needs fixing" from their own expertise, trust follows naturally.

That's why the best first AI project is always something familiar. If your office manager writes the same style of email twenty times a week, start there. They know what a good version looks like. They can judge the AI's draft instantly — no training required, no leap of faith. The AI earns credibility by performing well on a task the person already understands deeply.

This approach works for every role. A bookkeeper can evaluate whether an AI-generated expense categorization is correct. A project manager can judge whether an AI-drafted status update captures the key points. A salesperson can tell immediately whether a follow-up email hits the right tone. The domain expertise is already there. You're just giving people a new tool to apply it to.

The opposite approach — starting with something unfamiliar or highly technical — is where trust breaks down. If you ask people to evaluate AI outputs in a domain they don't understand well, they either over-trust (accepting everything without review) or under-trust (rejecting everything out of uncertainty). Neither outcome helps. You need the middle ground where people feel qualified to judge.

Over time, as confidence builds, you can expand to more complex workflows. But the foundation is always the same: start with what people know, let them see the AI perform, and let trust accumulate from experience rather than from promises. It's slower than a big-bang rollout, but it's the approach that actually sticks.
